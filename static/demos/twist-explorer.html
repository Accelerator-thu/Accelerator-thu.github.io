What a twist does to space

A twist ξ = (v, ω) is a velocity field on all of space. Hold it for time t and every point, including the rigid body, rides a helix around one shared screw axis.

A twist is a field, not an arrow at one place

Every point p of space gets the velocity vp = v + ω × p. Picture the body extended to fill all of space. Then ω is its angular velocity, the same everywhere, and v is the velocity of whichever body point happens to be passing through the origin right now. It is usually not the velocity of anything you can see, which is why it feels strange.

Every twist is a screw

Split v into a part perpendicular to ω and a part along it. The perpendicular part is fake: pick the reference point q = ω × v / |ω|² instead of the origin and it vanishes. The parallel part is real and cannot be moved away; its size is the pitch h = ω·v / |ω|². So any (v, ω) means: spin at rate |ω| about the line through q along ω, while sliding along that line at speed h|ω|. Read backwards, v = −ω × q + hω: the magenta arrow is the tangential speed the origin picks up from circling an offset axis, the violet arrow is the slide.

The exponential integrates the field

Holding ξ fixed for time t gives g(t) = exp(ξ̂ t) g(0). Every point traces a helix around the same axis with the same pitch; only the radius differs. Points on the axis just slide, so speed is smallest there, |h||ω|, and grows linearly with distance.

Things to try

Load the offset-axis preset. The body only rotates, yet v is nonzero. In the screw tab, drag q and watch v change while the motion stays a pure rotation.

In the twist tab with ω = (0, 0, 1), drag vz: that alone sets the pitch. Drag vx or vy: the axis moves sideways, the type of motion does not change.

Start from any screw and shrink |ω| toward zero with v fixed. The axis runs away to infinity. A pure translation is a rotation about an axis at infinity.

Conventions

This is the spatial twist, ordered ξ = (v, ω) as in Murray, Li and Sastry; Lynch and Park write V = (ω, v). The body twist Adg⁻¹ξ describes the same motion in body axes, and its v is the velocity of the body-frame origin. Changing frames changes v (it behaves like a moment) but never ω, and never the screw axis. The axis is the frame-independent object.
